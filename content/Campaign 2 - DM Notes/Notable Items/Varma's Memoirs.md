I have been told, by more people than I can now count, that I should write things down. That memory is unreliable. That the past deserves a witness. These are arguments made by people who have not yet understood that the past does not ~~care whether it is witnessed~~ — or rather: the past does not need a witness. It needs to be let go.

I am writing this anyway. Not because I have changed my opinion on the matter. Because I have been alive long enough to know that the things we refuse to do eventually do themselves, whether we sanction them or not.

I do not know what year it is as I begin. I have not counted years in a very long time. The [[Setting/Cultures/Solari|Solari]] have their [[Setting/Semantics/Dating/index|calendar]] now — thirteen months, twenty-eight days, anchored to the second resettlement of [[Solaum|a city]] on a river where my people once held court. I find I cannot bring myself to use it. There is something obscene about measuring the world’s age from the moment humans arrived at a place we had already abandoned.

That is an unkind thought. I will leave it.

――――――――――――

# i. the earliest things I am willing to put into words

I was not the oldest of us. I should say that first, because I have noticed that people assume it, and the assumption irritates me in ways I cannot fully justify. Sere was older. Miranu was older. Several others I will not name here, because to name them is to use up the last of them, and I am not ready to do that.

What I can say is that I was old enough to remember before [[The Convergence]]. Before the gods decided that the arrangement was no longer working and took their leave of it. I was old enough to have had opinions about whether they should.

I will not record those opinions here. They are still too sharp.

What the world was like before, I have been asked this by every curious student I have ever had, and I always give the same insufficient answer: fuller. That is the honest word for it. The world was fuller. Magic was not something you reached for; it was something you swam in. Every stone had weight that was not merely physical. Every tree had a voice that was not merely wind. The great cats of the eastern forest could hold a conversation with you if you were patient and they were in the mood, which they rarely were. The sky at night was not dark. It was lit from within by the movement of things that no longer move.

I am aware that this description sounds like the nostalgia of the very old. It probably is. But I have lived long enough on both sides of the Convergence to know that nostalgia, in this case, is simply accurate memory.

I wrote “never return to this” next to this passage the last time I read it. I am returning to it anyway. Some instructions are not worth following.

We were not gods. I want to be clear about that, because the records — what survive of them, filtered through six or seven generations of Chinese-whisper scholarship — tend to describe my race as divine or semi-divine, and this is not accurate. We were old, and we were capable of things that other peoples were not, and we moved between layers of the world that most beings cannot perceive. None of that is divinity. Divinity is something else. Divinity is what the gods had, and what they took with them, and what left a hole in the world when they ascended that nothing has fully filled since.

We served them. That is the honest way to say it. We served as intermediaries between the divine and the mortal, between the celestial currents and the physical plane, between the things that could not speak to each other directly and needed someone in between. Diplomats, the language of this age would say. Translators.

We were very good at it. And then the gods decided they no longer needed translators. And then we were no longer necessary. And then ~~and then the betrayal~~. ~~and then what they did to the Courts~~. ~~and then Miranu, and Sere, and the others I will not name~~.

I will come back to that. Or I will not. I have not decided.

# ii. the long middle, which is most of it

After the Convergence, I walked.

That is a simple sentence for an enormous span of time. I walked for longer than most civilisations have existed. I watched the peoples of this world emerge from the rubble of the pre-Convergence order — blinking, confused, reaching for a magic that no longer answered them, building fires because fire at least still worked. I watched [[Ebbu]] go up on the sand. I watched the first quarried stones of [[Itrea]] settle into the hillside. I watched children who had no word for what I was decide what word to use and arrive, inevitably, at some version of ghost.

I was not a ghost. I was a survivor. The distinction felt important at the time. It feels less important now.

I did not teach in those centuries. I did not want students. A student requires you to believe that what you know is worth passing on, and I was not convinced of that. I had spent an age passing things between the divine and the mortal and the celestial, and the result was: the gods left, my people were killed, and the world stripped itself of magic like a man pulling off a burning coat. I was not certain that my accumulated knowledge was an asset rather than a record of catastrophic failure.

I walked, and I observed, and I did not intervene. I watched the [[Qatha]] develop their calendar. I watched the long dark after the Convergence do its patient work of erasure. I watched the new peoples build their new stories about what the world had been and why it was now the way it was, and I noted, with what I chose to call scholarly detachment, how rarely the stories bore much resemblance to what I had actually seen.

At some point I stopped being angry about this. That was probably the most significant moment of the long middle period, and there was no one to witness it.

I walked past [[Solaum]] four times before it existed. Past the [[Atri]] River bend, past the stone that would later be the foundation of the [[Obelisk of Origin|Obelisk]] that housed the [[Power]] Element. I always found the site unsettled. Something in the ground there. Not corrupted — I would not use that word. Waiting.

There was a period, I will not say when, because the dates mean nothing to me and the specifics are private, when I tried to end it. Not in the sense that you might fear. I am not easily ended, and I knew it. But I made a serious effort to find whatever passes, for something like me, for a natural conclusion. I climbed the mountain. I sat in the highest silence. I waited for whatever had taken Miranu and Sere to come and take me.

It did not come.

I came back down the mountain. I decided, in the absence of any alternative, to find something to do with the time.

――――――――――――

# iii. the boy

I found him by accident, which is how the important things tend to arrive.

He was burning down a forest. Or beginning to. A small fire that had the ambition of a large one, feeding on itself with the particular enthusiasm of magic that has just discovered it exists. I could feel it from a considerable distance — not the heat, but the shape of it. The mana around him was rearranging itself in real time, folding toward a point that would, if left alone, either consume him or consume the forest or both.

He was sitting in the middle of it with his hands out, watching. Not afraid.

Watching.

That was the thing that stopped me. I have seen many people discover their elemental capacity for the first time. Terror is the usual response, or grief, or denial. He was watching it the way you watch a flame you have never seen before — with total, hungry attention. As if the most urgent thing in the world was to understand what it was doing and why.

I told him he was imbalancing the forest. He did not look up. He said, without turning: “I know. Tell me how to stop.”

Not “help me stop”. Not “can you stop it”. “tell me how.”

I had been alive for longer than I could accurately count, and in all of that time no one had ever made me want to laugh with that quality of involuntary, helpless, genuine surprise.

I named him Aetor. The One Who Knows. It was forward of me — a name is a promise, and I had not yet decided I was going to teach him anything. But the name came out before I could reconsider it, and he, and his people, softened it to [[Atiri]], and that was that. The name made the promise real.

I poured everything into him. That is the only honest way to say it. Forty-five years of teaching that was not merely instruction but evacuation — I was emptying myself of what I carried because I had finally found someone I trusted to carry it further. Every technique I had retained from before the Convergence. Every fragment of knowledge about the elements that the [[Aeia]] tradition had partially preserved and partially mangled. The old draconic tongues. The nature-magic that sings rather than commands. And the elemental arts, which are both simpler and more dangerous than any of the others, because they do not require you to reach outside yourself.

He was a difficult student. I want to say this. He argued with everything. He demanded justification for every rule. He wanted to understand the mechanism of each technique before he would consent to practise it, which is not how you learn to handle fire; you learn to handle fire by handling fire; you learn the mechanism afterward, when your hands have stopped shaking. We had this argument approximately three hundred times. He was wrong about the method. He was right about the underlying principle. I never admitted this to him while he was alive.

That last line is a lie. I admitted it on a night near the end, when I had begun to understand that the end was coming. He pretended not to hear me. He was merciful that way.

He could not feel [[Aushuzi]]. I want to record this separately because it troubled him, and because his interpretation of it — that the goddess did not come to him because he was too mortal, or too damaged, or somehow rejected — was wrong in a way I did not know how to correct without telling him things I had decided not to tell him.

Aushuzi did not come to him because Aushuzi, by the time Atiri was climbing that mountain, was a pattern in the architecture of the world rather than an entity capable of arrival. The gods did not withdraw to a place from which they might return. They became something else. What the Aeia tradition preserves as contact with the divine is, in the most honest analysis I can make, contact with the residue of divinity — the impression left by a foot that has long since lifted from the ground. I could feel it because I was tuned to the original, because I had spent my entire existence as an instrument calibrated to hear a note that is no longer played. Atiri could not feel it because there was nothing to feel.

I could not tell him this. He needed to believe the mountain held something. And in the way that mattered most — the way that made him keep climbing, keep asking, keep

wanting — it did.

The mountain taught him humility and endurance and loneliness and truth. Those are real things. They do not require a goddess to be real.

# iv. what I knew was coming

I knew before he did. I think I had known for several years before the end — that the teaching was concluding, that what I had to give was mostly given, that the shape of what came next was not going to include me standing in the forest calling him back to practice at dawn.

I did not tell him. This was deliberate and I stand by it, and I expect he would disagree if he could read this, and he would be partially right. But a teacher who tells a student “I am leaving soon” changes what the student learns in the time remaining. The last lessons become about the leaving rather than the work. I did not want that. I wanted the work to stay the work until the last possible moment.

What I gave him in those final years without naming them as final:

Everything I knew about the elements as they had been before the Convergence — not the diminished, post-Convergence version that the world had settled into, but the original, which is deeper and stranger and considerably more dangerous.

The nature of the Elemental Shards, and what the Obelisks are, and why they were built, and what lives inside them. The Aeia preserved the knowledge of how to build the containment. They did not preserve the knowledge of what they were containing or why. I gave him that.

The Convergence cycle. The interval. What the third one will require and why it will require it. This I gave him in the form of [[Atiric Texts|the Texts]] themselves — not as explicit instruction but woven through everything, so that the right reader at the right time would find it waiting. Atiri did not know he was recording it. He thought he was writing memoir.

A name. Not his. Not mine.

I will not put it here.

On the morning of the fifteenth of Narimi, I woke before him. I sat with the forest for a long time. I said the things that need to be said to the things that remember them.

Then I left.

I did not leave a body because I do not leave bodies. I did not leave a note because what I had to say was already in his hands, in the Texts, where it would wait as long as it needed to wait. I did not say goodbye because goodbye is a promise that the one leaving will be missed, and I did not want him to grieve what was not a death.

He searched for forty days. I watched him search for forty days. This was perhaps unkind. I am not certain I made the right decision. I am certain that if I had let him find me, he would not have gone to Solaum and built [[Atiri Centre of Magical Excellence|the school]], and everything that needed to follow from that would not have followed.

The school needed to exist. The Texts needed to be in a place where they would be found and hidden and found again.

Forty days of grief was the price. I paid it for him, watching from a distance, which is its own kind of cost.

# v. after

I have kept moving. That is the substance of everything after Atiri. I have kept moving, and I have watched, and I have made certain adjustments to the world’s arrangement when the adjustments were small enough to make without tipping into the kind of intervention that changes the nature of what I am trying to do.

The Texts have been lost three times and found four times. Twice I found them myself and moved them. The first time I cursed them with a spell that prevents it from opening except under the control of a Fire Elemental. The second I charmed them with a spell that will speak to the possessor so long as they are an elemental.  Once they were moved by someone else and I followed to make sure they ended up somewhere appropriate. The appropriate place changes depending on what is needed next. They are not meant to be a permanent archive. They are meant to be a delivery mechanism, and delivery mechanisms need to be in the right hands at the right moment, not in a vault where they will be preserved and ignored.

I watched [[Solara|the Empire]] rise. I watched [[Aelius]] make himself Emperor, which was the most extraordinary act of practical ambition I had seen in a thousand years, and I had seen considerable ambition in a thousand years. He was correct that it needed to happen. He was correct about most things, which I found irritating and also moving. He did not know about the cycle. He did not need to.

I watched the Empire fall, which took longer and was less dramatic than the rise, as falls usually are. [[1127 IOG - 1330 IOG - Freiburg Migration Period|The Migration Period]] looks catastrophic in retrospect; it felt, from the inside, like a slow tide that no one could find the will to turn back. The peoples involved were not villains. They were cold and hungry and moving toward warmth and food. The empire they dismantled had been built on practices that did not deserve permanence. The world that emerged was not better, precisely, but it was different, and different was what the Convergence cycle required.

There is a version of this paragraph that acknowledges my own culpability more directly. I wrote it. I burned it. I will not re-examine it here. If you are reading this and you know which specific events I am declining to discuss, you already know enough about them to make your own judgment, and your judgment does not require my assistance.

[[Stormshroud Legion|The Stormshroud]]. The decline of Solaum. The slow forgetting of everything the Republic had preserved of the old knowledge, which was itself only a fraction of what the Aeia had preserved, which was itself only a fraction of what existed before the Convergence. Each generation loses a little more. This is not entropy; it is preparation. A world that retained full memory of the previous Convergence would approach the next one with strategies and expectations that would make it worse. The forgetting is part of the mechanism.

I repeat this to myself as necessary. It has not yet stopped working entirely.

# vi. on the stones, briefly, because I find I cannot write at length about them

The stones are not my creation. I want to say that clearly because there is a version of events — I have heard fragments of it in the scholarship that has developed around the Convergence history — that implies that we, or more closely *I*, made the elemental artifacts. We did not make them. The Convergence made them. We understood them, which is different.

What I understand about them:

Each stone is a shard of what the relevant element was before it was diminished. Not a container. A remnant. The stone and the element are the same thing at different scales.

A stone does not choose its bearer. The stone and the bearer are in a relationship of resonance, not selection. When a stone finds the person whose elemental nature matches the frequency of the remnant, there is no choosing involved. It is like asking whether a bell chooses its note.

The [[Electricity]] element was destroyed in the [[700 IOG - Second Convergence|Second Convergence]]. This was not an accident or a tragedy in the way that the people who experienced it experienced it. It was a requirement of the next cycle. I am sorry for what it costs a world to lose a whole element. I am not sorry for the mechanism, which was necessary.

From eight to seven. Now, the seven have become the six. The six will, in the end, become something else. What it becomes depends on the Ascension. It has always depended on the Ascension.

I will not write more about this here. The Texts say what needs to be said about the Ascension. I have written in this journal long enough tonight.

# vii. what I miss, because apparently I am writing that kind of memoir tonight

Sere’s voice. She had a way of speaking that made everything she said sound like it was being said for the last time, with full awareness that it was the last time, and full acceptance of this. I spent a long time thinking this was affectation. It was not.

The way the world felt when the gods were present in it. Not their instructions. Not their demands. Not the Court rituals and the endless protocol of divine attendance. The feeling. The way the air had weight. The way certainty of a particular kind — not faith, not belief, something more sensory than either — was simply available.

Atiri arguing with me. I know this is sentimental. I am old enough to be sentimental without apology.

The eastern forests before the trade routes turned them to resources. The great cats who would not speak to you unless they felt like it, which they rarely did, which made it better when they did.

Not being the last. That is the one I circle back to most often, late in the night. It is not grief exactly; I have had too long to process the grief and whatever is left of it has changed into something I do not have an accurate name for. But not being the last would have been — would still be — something.

I have been called the last so many times by so many different people that I have almost started to believe it myself. Almost.

# viii. the present moment, which I am not going to explain

There are six of them now. More or less. One is already moving and does not yet know why. The stone found him in the mountains because the stone knows its own timing, not mine.

The Texts are where they need to be. The woman should have them now, and the Texts will do what the Texts do, and she, and hopefully the rest, will climb the mountain.

The mountain is patient. The mountain has waited this long.

I am aware that writing “I am not going to explain” in a heading and then continuing to write is precisely the kind of thing Atiri used to do, and precisely the kind of thing I used to criticise him for. I am leaving it anyway.

There is a boy in the city who is going to burn everything down, if he is not careful, and also if he is careful. Fire is like that. He does not yet know who he is and he does not yet need to. He has been surviving longer than he should have been able to and he does not know why. He will find out.

There is a woman walking away from an arrangement that was made without her knowledge for purposes she does not yet understand, carrying something in her hands that has been trying to reach her for more than a thousand years. The Texts found the right pair of hands. They always do, when I put them somewhere reasonable. I will take credit for that much.

There is a man who lifts impossible things and does not fully understand yet that lifting impossible things is the least of what he can do. The stone knew him. The stone knows.

There is a girl hiding who she is inside a borrowed title, and the earth knows her name even if the people around her do not.

There is a man made of grief who talks to the dead and is surprised when they answer. The dead have always answered people like him. He is simply the first in a long time who could hear it.

Ice seems to be gathering around a prince's stone cold heart and his catfire friend. She knows of this but he does not. It is the first time I have seen a shard choose two instead of one, maybe it is the remnant of electricity that causes the problem. It is a wonder why they haven't noticed they keep running into each-other.

And somewhere — I am choosing not to know where, for now — there is a wind that has not found its stone yet. That is the one I watch for. That is the one that determines whether this cycle resolves the way the previous ones did not.

I am going to the mountain soon. Not yet. There is one more thing to put in place first, and then I am going, and I will be there when they arrive, and I will be whatever I need to be when they arrive, and that is all I am going to say about it here.

This journal has been a document of what I remember. Let the record show: I remember almost everything. The things I have chosen to forget are chosen with purpose, and they remain in their place, and I do not regret the choice.

What I have poured into others has not diminished me in the way I once expected it to. Atiri was wrong about many things; he was right that knowledge is not power but perception. I gave him everything I knew and I still know it. It works like light, not like water. You do not have less of it because you have given some away.

That is a comfort. I note it here for the benefit of anyone who comes after and finds themselves in a similar position, which is unlikely, given that I am the last of a particular kind of person, but not impossible.

— V.
