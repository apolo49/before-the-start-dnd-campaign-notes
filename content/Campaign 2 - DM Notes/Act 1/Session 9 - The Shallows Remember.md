### Life on the Zoraneset (6th Hanomi, 06:00–14:00)

The session opens with the ship a day out of Pithertara. Give the table a breath before the combat — let the players establish their routines, their relationships with the reduced crew, the texture of being at sea.

_[[Riley]]_ — The bosun's job is below deck: oars, hull integrity, crew morale among the rowers. There's a rower named **[[Peles]]**, seventeen years old, who has started following Riley around with unconcealed admiration. He's from a coastal village the party will visit at the end of the session, though he doesn't mention it yet. He's nervous about approaching the southern shipping lanes and won't say why.

_[[Ellowen]]_ — The [[Atiric Texts]] spoke to her again last night. The session opens with the stone repeatedly talking about Water. Just the word. Over and over, a voice like pressure at depth: _ami, ami, ami_ — which is the Solari word for water. She can tell the party or not. The Quartermaster's charts show they're approaching a stretch of coast marked in old Imperial notation as _Moli Ithami_ — the Itami Sea's inner waters.

_[[Big Beefy Bob]]_ — First Mate's job: crew morale and discipline. Two rowers got into a fight overnight over rations. One of them says the sea smells wrong today. The other agrees, which is why they fought — neither wanted to say it first. Bob can investigate: a successful Perception or Nature check (DC 12) confirms it. The water around the ship is unusually clear. No fish. Not one.

_[[Yvaquis]]_ — As ship's surgeon she has been tending three rowers with lash wounds from the previous captain's regime. One of them, **Toras**, an older man, grew up on the Itami coast. If she builds rapport with him (any social roll, DC 10), he'll tell her: _"This stretch. The Shallows of Ithami. My grandfather said the deep things here have moods. Last year the fishing boats stopped coming back. Not all of them — just enough."_

_[[John Ironchest]]_ — He's been running a physical conditioning programme for the undermanned crew. During the morning exercise John notices [[Come-At-Me White]] is distressed in his below-deck stabling — pacing, refusing water. Horses are good barometers.

**[[Woubble]]** can be wherever seems funniest. [[Pupolas Crate]] has, inexplicably, befriended the ship's cat, which is a narrative problem for the ship's cat.

### The Warning (6th Hanomi, 14:00–14:30)

Give the players this in two beats, five minutes apart.

**Beat one:** One of the rowers on the port side shouts. Something just touched his oar from below. Not hit — _touched_. Deliberately, like a hand. He pulls his oar in. The stroke falters. Ellowen, on the quarterdeck, can see over the gunwale into the water and with a DC 12 Perception check makes out shapes below the surface. Many shapes. Moving with the ship.

**Beat two:** A figure rises from the water at the port bow and clings to the hull. It's a merfolk — but wrong. The eyes are clouded, a flat grey-white that should be dark. The gills are working too fast. The hands gripping the ship's timbers are bleeding from the clench. It opens its mouth and produces a sound that is almost language — the Raru traders who sometimes put in at Amphos use a clicking coastal dialect — but fractured, repeating the same syllable: _a broken word, looping, like a scratch on stone._

It does not attack. It is looking at the ship with those ruined eyes and it is terrified.

Then six more emerge behind it, and they are not terrified. They are furious.

### COMBAT — THE ATTACK ON THE SHALLOWS (14:30–16:30)

**Design philosophy:** This is a ship combat, not a dungeon encounter. Think in terms of three simultaneous problems rather than one monster to kill. Players are spread across a 120-foot vessel with 120 panicking crew. Their individual actions matter but so do their decisions about where to be and what to order.

**The three simultaneous problems:**

---

**PROBLEM 1: THE HULL BREACH** _Relevant: Riley, Yvaquis, any crew directed below_

Merfolk are using bone spikes and coral tools to attack the hull below the waterline at two points. They're not strong enough to sink the ship quickly, but they will compromise the oar ports and flood the lower rowing deck within 20 minutes without intervention.

Below decks: two breach points, one on each side, each requiring a DC 14 Strength check to brace with timber and pitch, or a DC 12 Dexterity check to jam with emergency materials. Two successes total plug the breaches. Failure on any one check means the flooding accelerates — reduce remaining time by 5 minutes. Yvaquis can direct rowers with a DC 13 Medicine/Athletics hybrid (she knows which men are strong enough, she's been treating them). Riley can use his Thieves' Tools to jury-rig a brace, DC 12.

The merfolk attacking the hull: four of them. They are not coming aboard. They are working, fast and focused, like sappers at a wall. They will fight if fought but their goal is the breach, not the crew.

---

**PROBLEM 2: THE BOARDING** _Relevant: Big Beefy Bob, John Ironchest, crew_

Twelve merfolk have pulled themselves over the gunwales onto the main deck. They are attacking rowers — specifically targeting the oarsmen, not the officers. They want the ship dead in the water. If they take out enough rowers, [[The Zoraneset]] cannot manoeuvre away from the shallows, and she'll eventually be driven onto the rocks by the current.

**Stat block — Frenzied Merfolk (modified):** Use standard Merfolk stats (MM p.218) with the following changes: advantage on attack rolls while within 5 feet of another merfolk, but disadvantage on Wisdom saving throws. They will not retreat. They will fight until unconscious. The grey-white eyes glow faintly when they're hit hard — not with light, but with something like heat.

Bob as First Mate can use his action to direct crew (DC 12 Persuasion/Intimidation) — on a success, four crew members take coordinated attacks against one merfolk this round, each with +3 to hit for 1d6 bludgeoning. On a failure, two crew members are overwhelmed and need rescuing next round.

John Ironchest on an open deck against twelve merfolk is, frankly, in his element. Let him be terrifying. The Power Stone should pulse when he hits one — they react to it. One merfolk stops its attack on a rower and turns to face John directly with something that looks, briefly, like recognition.

---

**PROBLEM 3: THE BALLISTAS AND THE SIPHON** _Relevant: Ellowen, any player who takes the quarterdeck_

The Zoraneset has two ballistas on the aft deck and the fire siphon at the prow. Someone needs to direct these, because the crew at the ballistas are trained gunners but they've never fired on merfolk at close range on their own ship — they're waiting for an officer to make the call.

**The fire siphon** is devastating. It can clear a 30-foot cone of burning naphtha, dealing 3d8 fire damage (DC 13 Dex save for half) to everything in the area, including the ship. A player at the prow can fire it — but they will need to be careful about where the crew are standing. Used well, one blast ends Problem 2. Used carelessly, the party will be putting out fires on their own ship.

**The ballistas** are better for Problem 1 — a bolt fired into the water beside the hull will scatter the merfolk working on the breach. Each requires a ranged attack roll (+5 to hit) against AC 12 (underwater targets). Hit: 3d10 piercing, kills one, scatters the others (they lose one breach action next round).

**Ellowen's Water Stone:** The stone has been growing warm since the shapes appeared under the water. If Ellowen uses a spell while holding it this combat, there's a side effect: the clouded eyes of the merfolk nearest her _clear_ for one second. Not all of them. Just the ones nearest her. She should notice this.

---

**THE LEADER — SHALETH**

At the midpoint of the combat (after roughly 6 rounds, or when two of the three problems have been addressed), something larger surfaces astern.

**Shaleth** is a merfolk elder — if merfolk can age, she is old. She is not grey-eyed. Her eyes are clear, dark blue, and she looks at the party with intelligence and fury and something underneath both that might be desperation. She carries a staff of deepwater coral wrapped in what appears to be kelp but is, on close inspection, woven script — the same clicking language, made visible.

She does not attack. She surfaces and she _watches_.

If a player attempts to communicate (any language, DC 15 — DC 10 if Ellowen uses the Water Stone, which seems to help), Shaleth can produce three words of Solari, heavily accented: _"Stone. It. Hurts."_

She means the elemental disturbance. She cannot explain it. She doesn't have the language. But the party now has a data point: the merfolk attack isn't aggression. It's pain, and the pain is connected to the elements.

Shaleth retreats when the combat ends or when the party clearly attempts communication over combat. The grey-eyed merfolk, without her presence, lose cohesion — they don't retreat cleanly, they just... stop pressing. Some slip back into the sea. Some need to be restrained or finished. The party makes their own calls.

---

### THE AFTERMATH (16:30–18:00)

**Damage report:** The DM should decide the hull damage based on how well Problem 1 was managed. If it was addressed quickly: superficial damage, no speed penalty. If it went badly: one oar bank is flooded, speed halved until repaired. Repair requires putting in to shore — which is actually useful, because that's where they're going.

**Rower casualties:** Two to four rowers are badly hurt (unconscious, overboard, or dead) depending on how Problem 2 went. The crew's morale takes a hit. The crew who were saved by player intervention are visibly grateful. Peles, the young rower who grew up near the end-of-session settlement, is one of the survivors if the players fought well — let them notice that.

**The first merfolk they saw** — the terrified one with the clouded eyes who arrived first and did not attack — is still clinging to the port hull. When the combat ends, it is still there. It is trembling. If anyone reaches down to it, it flinches but doesn't release its grip. It has been holding on to the ship this entire time. It doesn't look dangerous. It looks exhausted.

What the players do with this creature is their choice. It cannot communicate beyond emotional expression. If they bring it aboard, Woubble attempts to befriend it immediately, which is a good tone-break after a hard combat. It will be important later.

**Bob's Dead Dreams note:** If Bob rests during the voyage later, the First Puli has something to say about what just happened — something about the sea having a long memory and elements having weight.

### ARRIVAL — TEPHROS (7th Hanomi, 08:00)

The ship makes the coast by dawn the following morning, driven by necessity (hull damage, wounded crew, exhausted rowers) and by Ellowen's charts showing a small settlement on the next navigable stretch of shore.

**Tephros:**

Population 2,100. A former Imperial provincial harbour — you can see the bones of what it was in the quayside stonework, the crumbling customs house, the grid of streets that was once wider. The Imperial design had capacity for 12,000. What remains uses maybe a third of that infrastructure, the rest colonised by goats, sea grass, and enterprising families who've knocked through walls to make compound living.

The economy: fishing, small-scale trade with the river settlements inland, and a dwindling timber trade from the forested hills behind the town. They used to export salt. The salt trade stopped eight months ago when the merfolk started attacking the salt beds at the shallow coast.

**The Archon:** Tephros is governed by **Archon Vaela Murso** — a woman in her late fifties, former Imperial customs administrator, now the de facto head of a settlement that has been bleeding people to merfolk attacks for eight months. She is not panicking. She is past panicking and into the cold management of ongoing disaster. When the Zoraneset pulls into her harbour, she is at the dock in person.

She has questions before she has welcome: _Who are you? Where from? What happened to your hull? Did you come through the Shallows of Ithami?_

When she hears they came through the Shallows and survived and have a merfolk on board, she pauses for a long moment and then says: _"You'd better come in."_

---

### TEPHROS — KEY LOCATIONS AND ENCOUNTERS

**The Harbourmaster's Hall** (former customs house, now town hall): Vaela spreads charts on the table. Eight months ago, the merfolk were a neutral presence — they sometimes traded shells and deep-water materials with the fishing boats, a non-verbal but functional relationship. Then something changed. The attacks began at the new moon. The salt workers were first. Then the fishing boats. Two weeks ago, a merfolk came ashore at night — not to attack, but to _look_ at the town. The night watch found it standing in the market square at third bell, staring at the Solari-era monument in the centre (a broken column with an inscription nobody can fully read anymore). It didn't attack. It left at dawn.

Vaela wants the attacks to stop. She does not know why they started. She does not have the resources to fight back — the town guard is twelve people. She has been hoping the problem would solve itself. It has not.

**The Monument:** The broken column in the market square. If any player investigates (DC 13 Arcana or History), the inscription is Imperial Solari, archaic register, partial: _"...the element of the sea requires no monument, as it builds its own."_ The column was erected during the reign of Puli Numini — the second Arksi, Bob's Dead Dreams contact. It is 1,700 years old. Something has scratched at the base of it recently. The scratches are not random. They are the same clicking script pattern from Shaleth's staff.

**Peles:** The young rower leads the players to his family here. His mother, **Demi**, runs the only functioning inn (three rooms, communal table, genuinely excellent fish stew). She recognises the situation with the merfolk merfolk from old stories her grandmother told — "when the sea holds a grievance, it isn't against _us_, it's against something _in_ the water." She doesn't know what that means in practical terms, but she's been trying to tell Vaela for months and Vaela is not a stories person.

**The Salt Beds:** A short walk along the coastal path, the former salt production area. Shallow evaporation pools, currently abandoned. At low tide, the bottom of the pools is visible and covered in coral-scratch writing — much more extensive than the monument. The merfolk have been writing here. Extensively. Whatever they're trying to communicate, this is where they've been trying to communicate it. It needs translation.

**The Merfolk Aboard the Zoraneset:** If the party brought the grey-eyed merfolk with them, it becomes animated when it sees the town. Not aggressive — urgent. It tries to move toward the salt beds. If allowed, it leads the party there, presses its hands into the writing on the pool floor, and looks at them with those clouded eyes. The clouds in its eyes, in direct sunlight, are not grey. They are faintly purple. The same purple as the disrupted electricity element.

### THE REVELATION THE PLAYERS CAN PIECE TOGETHER

This session doesn't deliver the answer — it delivers enough pieces that the party can assemble a hypothesis:

The merfolk in the Shallows of Ithami are experiencing something analogous to what Ellowen experienced in the water plane — an elemental disturbance radiating from... somewhere nearby. The salt beds are on a ley line. The monument is on the same ley line. The Shallows of Ithami are on the edge of it.

The merfolk aren't attacking because they're hostile. They're attacking because the elemental disruption is causing something like madness — the grey-white eyes are the symptom — and they can feel that it's coming from the direction of the ships passing through. They can't distinguish between the source and the vessels near it. The attacks are, in their way, a desperate attempt to stop something they can't describe.

What's causing the disruption is not in Tephros. It's further south. Toward Itrea. Toward Mount Aemi.

Vaela will provision the ship and let them use her drydock for basic hull repairs if they can either explain the merfolk situation in terms she finds credible, or make concrete progress on stopping the attacks. She doesn't need the attacks ended tonight. She needs someone who understands what's happening — she's been operating in the dark for eight months. That's enough.

---

### ENDING THE SESSION

Two possible endings depending on time and energy:

**Hard stop:** The party has made port, met Vaela, seen the salt beds, and has enough to discuss. End on the image of Peles showing Ellowen the scratch writing in the salt beds at low tide, the sun going down behind the hills, and the sound of Tephros going quiet for the first time in months — because, for one night, the sea is calm.

**Soft ending with a hook:** As the party settles into the inn for the night, Vaela mentions, almost as an aside, that a traveller came through Tephros three weeks ago — _"strange person, older than they looked, asked a great many questions about the old column, left before dawn, didn't pay for the room"_ — and left something: a single line of Solari written on the table of the room they occupied, which Demi kept because it seemed important. The note reads: _"The column remembers. Ask it what it saw before you climb."_

No signature. Ellowen's Water Stone is warm against her hand.

### DM NOTES

**Shaleth:** She shouldn't be killed if it can be helped. She should be available for future contact if the party wants it. She is the one coherent intelligence in the merfolk situation and she will remember the ship that tried to talk.

**The Electricity connection:** The purple in the grey-eyed merfolk's eyes is a deliberate plant — the destroyed Electricity element is leaving traces in beings sensitive to the elemental world. Don't confirm this. Let it sit.

**Rhoda:** The Shimmering Moontide is somewhere in these waters. No Rhoda appearance this session — but Tephros's oldest fisherman, if anyone talks to him, says he once saw a creature that was half person and half something else, flying low over the water at dawn, near the outer reef. _"Sang something. Changed the wind. Then gone."_ That's your plant.

**The merfolk aboard:** If they kept the grey-eyed merfolk, it should go back to the sea when the ship enters harbour — slipping over the side quietly, without drama. If anyone watches it go, it pauses in the water and looks back once, and the clouds in its eyes are almost entirely gone.
