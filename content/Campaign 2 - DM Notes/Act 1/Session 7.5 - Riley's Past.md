# Premise

[[Riley]] is going about on his normal activities in [[Amphos]] when he is suddenly apprehended by the city watch, leased and beat. He was sentenced to execution when suddenly the city guards let him go under orders of the head of the military [[Zenshoran Hagashar]] because "He's not worth it, he's a street rat, there's not enough evidence, everyone said it was a girl half-tabaxi, not a boy."

# Scenes

- **The Zoraneset**
    - **The Lower Deck/Stairs**: Where the Captain’s body was discovered at 05:00 AM.
    - **The Supplies Cabinet**: Found wide open near the murder site, suggesting it was tampered with during the incident.
    - **The Captain’s Quarters**: A focal point of the investigation where tracks and evidence eventually lead.
    - **Crew Bunks**: The location where stolen spirits (alcohol) were used in a botched cleanup of the crime scene.
- **The Port City**: A tiny settlement of approximately 4,000 people where the ship eventually docks.

# Story

[[Dating|1st Ebolumi 1778 IOG - 3rd Ebolumi 1778 IOG]]

### The Crime
1st Ebolumi 1778 IOG, 05:00 - 07:30

[[Wrensu]] informs Riley that the military are moving an important artifact holding secrets and tells him that he has to make the steal. It is currently stored in the [[The Great Palace of Amphos]], in their archive. He must lift the papers then deliver the message to his merchant contact who will pay him in kind. If Riley askes why him he responds:
> Riley is the courier because he's small, fast, and passes as a girl. He's done it a hundred times.

This time, a City Watch officer intercepts the delivery. Not because they're watching Riley — because they're watching the _recipient_. The recipient bolts. The papers are found on Riley. Riley cannot read these papers because they are written in an old tongue by someone who was very well educated. It appears to be ripped from a memoir of some sort. It is clearly a precious artefact that would belong to the [[Magic Academy of Amphos]] and the [[Head of the Arcane]]. Possession of this is not a theft charge. It is a treason charge. He bolts

This is important because:

- Riley genuinely doesn't know what he was carrying
- He can't explain it without implicating Wrensu
- The Watch has real cause — it isn't just an overzealous arrest
- The sentence of death is proportionate to the crime they believe he committed

### The Arrest
1st Ebolumi 1778 IOG, 08:00 - 08:30

[[Riley]] is on the road between the [[Northern Slums]] market and a contact's house in the lower merchant district. He's in his usual presentation — feminine, small, catlike, invisible. The market is loud. He doesn't notice the two plainclothes officers until they're already either side of him.

Key beats:

- The arrest happens fast — no combat, just numbers and surprise
- They call him "girl" and "cat-girl" during the initial grab. He doesn't correct them.
- The papers are found in the lining of his bag
- He's cuffed in regular iron restraints (he's not known to be an elemental; no suppression cuffs)
- He's taken to the City Watch precinct in the merchant district, not the slums — this is already out of the ordinary

For the player:
Let them attempt escape — one roll. Make it feel possible. Then shut it. There are six officers and a crowded street where running makes you look guilty and being caught running with military papers makes you a spy, not a thief. The Watch wants him alive for now because they want to know who his contacts are.

### Interrorgation
1st Ebolumi 1778 IOG, 10:30 - 29:00

Riley is held overnight. This is where the session slows down and you let the player just _exist_ in the situation.

**The cell:** Stone, damp, one barred window too high to reach. Three other prisoners — a drunk (Barudatos), an old man who doesn't speak (Alibos), and a young woman (Dolashaka) who watches Riley very carefully and eventually says: _"You're not a girl... are you..."_ Not a question. She isn't hostile. She's just been in here long enough to notice.

**The interrogation:** An officer named **Paros Atat** — 40s, tired, bureaucratic, not cruel but not kind — asks how Riley came into possession of the papers, where he was taking them, who pays him. Riley knows nothing useful. He knows Wrensu's name and face and nothing else. Atat doesn't believe him. A man doesn't have to be cruel to apply systematic pressure; Atat just keeps asking.

**DM note on Wrensu:** He knows Riley is taken. His underground contacts told him within two hours. He's trying to get Riley out through back channels — calling in debts, talking to people. But he can't move fast enough and he can't burn his cover doing it. The player doesn't know any of this yet. Let them feel alone.

**The charge:** On the morning of the 2nd, Riley is formally charged. Not theft. Espionage against the Eastern Solaran Empire. The sentence, under Imperial law, is death by public execution in the forum square. The execution is scheduled for the following morning.

### The Night Before
2nd Ebolumi 1778 IOG

This is the session's emotional centre. Riley is moved to a holding cell with three other condemned prisoners. The executions in the morning will be a small batch — seven people, various crimes.
What does Riley do with one night?
Let the player sit with it. Some things you can offer:

- The young woman (Dolashaka) from before is also in this cell. She stole grain from a military storehouse to feed her family. She's been in the system longer than Riley and is grimly calm about it. She's not looking for hope. She asks Riley to tell her something true. ("Not brave. Not 'it'll be alright.' Something true.") — this is a conversation, let it breathe
- A guard brings water. Riley notices the guard's hands are shaking. The guard is young — younger than Riley looks. He doesn't make eye contact. He's more frightened than Riley
- Riley can attempt to pick the lock on his cell. The roll is extremely difficult. If he fails, nothing happens — the guard doesn't notice but the other prisoners watch him. If somehow he succeeds, have him make it two corridors before a different guard stops him cold and brings him back. He gets hit for it. Not badly — a blow to the ribs — but it establishes that escape isn't available tonight.

#### The Flagellation
This doesn't happen at execution dawn. In Imperial procedure, prisoners facing capital charges are flogged on the eve of execution — partly punitive, partly a kind of preparation rite. Twenty-two lashes, administered in the courtyard at second bell of evening. Riley is brought out, strapped to a post, and flogged in front of the other condemned.
Why 22 specifically? Because the officer administering punishment stops at 22 when a runner arrives with a message from the precinct commander. The flogging is interrupted. Riley is cut down and returned to the cell. Nobody explains why.

### The Release
3rd Ebolumi 1778 IOG, 04:00 - 05:30

The executions are due at first light. The other six condemned are taken from the cell. Riley is not. A different officer — older, wearing the uniform of the Tagaminase rather than the City Watch — arrives at the cell. He doesn't introduce himself.

He reads from a document. The language is formal, Imperial Aeikas:
> _Insufficient evidence of wilful participation in espionage. Witness statements describe a girl, half-Tabaxi. The accused is a boy. The accused is released. The accused is a street rat. There is nothing here._

He says the last three sentences not reading from the document. His own addition. He folds the paper. He looks at Riley once — _really_ looks, the kind of look that inventories a person — and then looks away. He gestures for the cell to be unlocked.

On a successful dc10 perception check Riley will spot the officer's sigil: a sun above a crossed blade and scroll. The mark of the Donpotes da Tagaminase's office. If Riley later hears the name [[Zenshoran Hagashar]], he has no reason to connect it to this moment. He doesn't know who the Donpotes is. He's fifteen and he's never had reason to learn.

He's walked to the precinct door and released onto the street. His bag — minus the papers — is returned. His knives are not. His lute is still at home.

### Epilogue
3rd Ebolumi 1778 IOG, 07:00 -

Riley walks home. You can play this as a montage if the player is emotionally tired, or you can walk it beat by beat if they want to decompress.

Wrensu is at the house. He knows Riley is out — his contacts told him before Riley was even on the street. He's sitting at the table with his hands around a cup that has gone cold. When Riley comes through the door, Wrensu doesn't move for a moment. Then he stands up and goes to check the wounds on Riley's back. He doesn't ask what happened in the cell. He doesn't ask about the papers. He works in silence, cleaning the lash marks.

At some point Riley can push for answers. What were those papers? Why did someone with the Tagaminase sigil release him?

Wrensu's answers, and you decide how much to give:

_"The papers were nothing. They weren't mine. I shouldn't have used you."_ — This is a partial lie. They were Wrensu's. He's protecting Riley by claiming otherwise.

_"The Tagaminase? I don't know who intervened."_ — This is also a partial lie. Wrensu doesn't know for certain it was Zenshoran, but he suspects. He has suspected, quietly, for years, that someone in the military apparatus has been keeping Riley off the worst lists. He doesn't know why and he's never wanted to know.

He then says something that he means:

_"You can't be the Freedom Cat anymore. Not the way you've been doing it. They've seen your face now. Find a different way to be yourself."_

He means it. He's frightened. He doesn't say frightened — he says it like an order — but the player should be able to feel what it is.
