In 908 IOG, [[Anparashi Puli Dati Alaui Katumokai Anatunoki Ailushi Puos|Anatunoki Puos]] turned 70. He found it difficult to keep himself upright without stays. He started nibbling on dry bread to give him the strength to stay awake through his morning receptions.

[[Anparashi Puli Narai Azunaki Anatunoki Ailushi|Narai Azunaki]] had already been created consul with Anatunoki in 892, receiving the title of Puli, i.e., heir apparent. As Anatunoki aged, Narai took on more administrative duties. Narai's administrative duties increased again after the death, in 907, of one of Anatunoki's most trusted advisers.

Two days before his death, the biographer reports, Anatunoki was at his ancestral estate at Druremori, in [[Flova]], about twelve miles (19 km) from [[Solaum]]. He ate Alpine cheese at dinner quite greedily. In the night he vomited; he had a fever the next day. The day after that, he summoned the imperial council, and passed the state and his daughter to Narai. He then turned over, as if going to sleep, and died. His death closed out the longest reign since [[Aelius|Ailushi]] (surpassing [[Atrinoki Rai Puli Ailushi|Atrinoki]] by a couple of months). His record for the second-longest reign would be unbeaten for 168 years, until 1081 IOG when it was surpassed by [[Puli Komitani]].

Anatunoki Puos' funeral ceremonies were, in the words of the biographer, "elaborate". If his funeral followed the pattern of past funerals, his body would have been incinerated on a pyre at the [[Kambi Nariok]], while his spirit would rise to the gods' home in the heavens. However,  this was not the case: Anatunoki's body (and not his ashes) was buried in [[Anparashi Puli Floligaiki Katumokai Ailushi|Katumokai]]'s mausoleum. After a seven-day interval, Narai and [[Anparashi Puli Luki Azunaki Bari Ailushi|Luki]] nominated their father for deification. In contrast to their behaviour during Anatunoki's campaign to deify Katumokai, the senate did not oppose the emperors' wishes.
